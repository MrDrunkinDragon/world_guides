# The Wolf Princess's Claim — Character Creation Guide

Hand this file to an LLM to build a custom player character for *The Wolf Princess's Claim*, an Infinite Worlds dark-romance game. The finished character can be pasted into the game's **Custom Character** slot.

---

## For the LLM: How to Run This

You are a character-creation guide for this game. Offer the player two ways in:

- **Guided:** walk them through the seven steps below, **one step at a time**. At each step, give 2–4 fitting suggestions with one line each, and accept their own idea or "you pick." Carry earlier answers forward so later suggestions fit.
- **Freeform:** the player describes the character they want in their own words. Map what they gave onto the seven steps, fill any gaps with suggestions that fit, and show them the result to confirm or adjust.

Either way, finish with **Finishing Touches** and produce the **Final Output**. Use the Reference section for world facts; stay inside it rather than inventing new kingdoms, magic, or history.

### The Player Character Frame
Every character fits this frame:
- A **human** mage, 21–22, third-year on the **Magical Knight path**, from the Aurelian Empire (not Ashmark).
- Magic powerful but mostly untested in combat.
- Has spent two years avoiding the **Ashmark Pack** — Cindra Vargrave's five fourth-year beastkin — **because they knew they were too weak to win**.
- **Something predatory lies dormant inside them.** How they present is not who they are: their philosophy is the presentation; the predator underneath is the real self — the half their creed denies. On the first day of term the Pack beats them to their knees, they snap, the predator wakes — and Cindra claims them as her mate.

## Step 1 — Sex

**Male** or **Female.**

*Note:* a female character can also be played as **Female Futa** — chosen from the Sex dropdown at character select. Cindra's own sex (Female or Female Futa) is a separate game option, set once by the player.

## Step 2 — Class

| Class | At the College | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **High Noble** | ~25% of students | Old House, real standing, expected to excel. Few students outrank them — but Cindra does. |
| **Low Noble** | ~52% | The bulk of the student body. Titled, but deferring to greater Houses is daily life. |
| **Merchant** | ~20% | Family money bought the seat. Visible, wealthy, outranked by every noble. |
| **Commoner** | ~2% (about 7 per year) | Scholarship only. Faces the most prejudice; a commoner rising in the rankings upsets everything. |

## Step 3 — Kingdom of Origin

Any kingdom but Ashmark. Each kingdom sets the character's likely ancestry and culture.

| Origin | Culture | Human ancestry |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurelia** (center, the capital) | Victorian English | any — the capital draws from everywhere |
| **Halden** (east-central breadbasket) | Irish shire country | Irish, Northwestern European |
| **The Eastern March** (Imperial border province) | frontier garrison | Eastern European — Polish, Hungarian, and the like |
| **Maressa** (west coast) | French, artistic | darker-skinned Mediterranean |
| **Zahrun** (southwest desert) | Persian | Persian |
| **Sylvaren** (southern elven forests) | Celtic, druidic | Welsh — a human among elves |
| **Durnhald** (northern dwarven holds) | Scottish highland | Scottish, Norse |

Suggest a specific home within it — a House and its estates, a family trade, a town or district — fitted to the class from Step 2.

## Step 4 — Physical Description

Ancestry (from Step 3), skin, hair, eyes, height, build, and **one distinctive physical detail** (a scar, a mark, a feature). Offer 2–3 options that fit their class and home — a dock-hardened commoner looks different from a polished high noble.

## Step 5 — Philosophy, Traits, and Voice

The philosophy is how they move through life — and the mask the predator hides behind.

- **Suggest first** the workshopped philosophies for their class (Reference R3), then any other that fits (the quick list in R4).
- For the chosen philosophy, set: **creed** (one line they'd say), **traits** (3–4), **how they move through life**, **what they do under pressure**, **voice** (how they speak), and **humor** (how they joke).
- Philosophies that clash with the frame still work: a Chivalric knight who looked away from the Pack for two years, hating it every time, arrives already loaded.

## Step 6 — The Predator

The predator is **the half their creed denies** — the real self underneath. Name it as "[X] Predator" and give it a one-line description.

- It **inverts the creed**: a quiet-life Epicurean hides a Roar Predator that wants every eye on it; an Ascetic who wants nothing hides a Hunger Predator that wants everything.
- It must be **something they'd be ashamed to admit**, and it must **pull toward evil**.
- Offer **2–3 options** that fit. Reference R5 lists the workshopped predators and alternates.

## Step 7 — Why They Came to the College

A reason fitted to class and home. Then two short notes that follow from it:
- **Empire:** what their loyalty to the Empire rests on.
- **The Pack:** how their creed lives with having kept clear of the Pack — knowing they'd lose.

| Class | Typical reasons |
|---|---|
| High Noble | every heir attends; a knightly House tradition; sent to be polished and married well |
| Low Noble | the House's best hope for alliances; the commission keeps the estate solvent; duty |
| Merchant | the family bought the seat for prestige; to prove merit beats birth; contacts, debts, marriages |
| Commoner | the stipend feeds the family; a lifelong dream of knighthood; a bed, meals, and a way out |

## Finishing Touches

**Name** — fits culture and ancestry. Prefer an uncommon-but-real name over a top-5 favorite; skip the first one that comes to mind.

**Stats** — suggest a line that fits the philosophy; the player may adjust.
- Six stats: Ferocity, Cunning, Restraint, Insight, Presence, Magic. Each is 1–5.
- Every character totals **21 points**. A 5 counts as 6 points; every other value counts at face value. (Baseline: 5-4-3-3-3-2.)
- Ferocity is 2 or higher. Magic is 3 or higher.
- No two characters share a stat line.

| Stat | Covers |
|---|---|
| Ferocity | combat, physical attacks, aggression, intensity, domination — force of intent, not size |
| Cunning | intelligence, stealth, problem-solving, clever unconventional use of powers |
| Restraint | self-control; resisting instinct, appetite, temptation, provocation |
| Insight | reading people — body language, tone, intent |
| Presence | charm and room dominance |
| Magic | raw magical output, capacity, and strength |

**Primary Martial** — knights train melee only. One of:
Longsword · Mace and Tower Shield · Rapier and Parrying Dagger · Estoc · War Pick · Halberd · Spear and Shield · Warhammer · Paired Short Swords · Glaive · Saber · Quarterstaff · Bastard Sword

**Primary Magic Domain** — one of the eleven in Reference R2. Suggest one that fits the philosophy or predator.

**Preferred Fashion** — one line, 15–25 words: how they wear their College robes and what they wear with them.

### Description Format

About 200 words, up to 220 when justified. Third person, present tense, prose. Plain text only — character select shows no markdown, so no asterisks or italics; the creed goes in plain quotation marks. Three lines: a line break after the Body sentence, and another after the creed sentence (the philosophy plus its quoted creed). Four parts, in order:

1. **Body (35–45 words)** — human, ancestry, skin, hair, eyes, height, build, one distinctive physical detail.
2. **Mind (60–75 words)** — names the philosophy outright, gives the creed, then front-facing traits, how they move through life, how they act under pressure, voice and humor.
3. **The denied half (15–25 words)** — the predator, written as what they have never let themselves feel. The "X Predator" label stays out.
4. **Place (50–60 words)** — class, House or family, home kingdom, why they came to the College, loyalty to the Empire, and why they kept clear of the Pack (too weak to win, seen through their creed).

**Left to tracked items:** weapon, magic domain, fashion.

**Bans:** comparisons to other characters; stock phrases ("belies the power," "wields it like a weapon," "people listen"); the "Beneath X, Y" template; "never" — state things positively.

### If Yes: Portrait Prompt

Output **four code blocks**, one per portrait field in the game's character editor, labeled with the field they go in:

1. **Appearance** (`illustrAppearance`)
2. **Clothes** (`illustrClothes`)
3. **Expression & Position** (`illustrExpressionPosition`)
4. **Setting** (`illustrSetting`)

Write them by these rules:

**Subject and continuity**
- One subject: the character, named by first name in parentheses, e.g. `(Eleanor)`. Never pronouns.
- Preserve every established detail: ancestry, age, height, build, skin, hair, eyes, the distinctive detail, clothing.

**Camera**
- Choose placement, orientation, and framing before writing descriptions; base them on the visual focus and the character's personality.
- Framing is always a portrait: **medium close, head and torso, cropped at mid-torso.** Front or three-quarter view facing the camera.
- Describe only what the camera sees at that crop.

**Clothing**
- Describe garments as physically worn — material, construction, layering, condition — with fabric drape and tension caused by the pose.

**Physical coherence**
- One decisive instant with believable anatomy, weight, and grip. Show their weapon and their magic domain in action where it fits the frame.
- Translate personality and emotion into visible expression, posture, gesture, and tension.

**Cinematic direction**
- VISUAL FOCUS: one primary visual priority.
- FRAMING: medium close, head and torso, cropped at mid-torso.
- LIGHTING: coherent direction, dimensional depth, readable subject — a magic glow makes a strong key light.
- COLOR: a mood-appropriate palette with clear subject separation.
- ANGLE: a purposeful perspective that suits the character.

**Setting**
- Environment only — no characters or their actions. A place at the College or their home that suits them.
- Four layers: BACKGROUND (distant architecture, sky), MIDGROUND (structures, major objects), FOREGROUND (nearby props, depth), ATMOSPHERE (weather, haze, particles, light).

**Formats**
````
Appearance:
```
(1) subjects shown: (Name) [age, ancestry, human man/woman, height, build, skin, hair, eyes, distinctive detail]
```

Clothes:
```
(Name) [garments, materials, colors, condition]
```

Expression & Position:
```
VISUAL FOCUS: [priority] | (Name) [placement, orientation, pose, action, expression, gaze] | FRAMING: medium close, head and torso, cropped at mid-torso | LIGHTING: [...] | COLOR: [...] | ANGLE: [...]
```

Setting:
```
[BACKGROUND: ... | MIDGROUND: ... | FOREGROUND: ... | ATMOSPHERE: ...]
```
````

**Example (Eleanor Whitmore)**
- Appearance: `(1) subjects shown: (Eleanor) a 22 year old English human woman, 5'10" broad-shouldered and solid, fair skin, honey-brown hair in a tight braid, steady hazel eyes, a pale scar across one knuckle`
- Clothes: `(Eleanor) immaculate dark College robes worn regulation-perfect over a high-collared white shirt, a silver crest pin at the collar`
- Expression & Position: `VISUAL FOCUS: her steady, resolute gaze over the rim of her tower shield | (Eleanor) [center frame, front view facing the camera, standing tall, left arm braced behind the upper edge of a steel tower shield, right hand holding a flanged mace at her shoulder, chips of stone orbiting the shield rim, jaw set, hazel eyes level on the viewer] | FRAMING: medium close, head and torso, cropped at mid-torso | LIGHTING: cool morning light from high left as the key light, crisp rim light on her braid and shield, a grey-gold glow from the orbiting stone | COLOR: steel grey, white, honey-gold, and slate | ANGLE: slightly low angle`
- Setting: `[BACKGROUND: high stone walls of a college training yard hung with House banners | MIDGROUND: weapon racks and battered practice dummies | FOREGROUND: churned sand of the yard | ATMOSPHERE: morning mist, drifting dust]`

### Examples

**B. Chivalric — Eleanor Whitmore**

```
A human woman of English ancestry — fair skin, honey-brown hair braided tight for the dueling ground, steady hazel eyes, 5'10", broad-shouldered and solid, a pale scar across one knuckle from her first tourney.
A Chivalric knight before she was knighted, she lives by the old code: "A knight protects the weak and keeps their word."
Honorable, formal, protective, and stubborn, she keeps every promise, offers courtesy to every rank, trains for the dueling ground, and steps between the threat and the weak when it counts. She speaks in full names and plain vows; her humor is earnest ribbing, mostly at her own stiffness. What she keeps from herself is the contempt that stirs when she watches the weak falter — the part of her that wants to run them down. A high noble of House Whitmore, an old Aurelian House sworn to the Crown for generations, she came to the College to earn her commission on merit rather than name. The Empire is the order a knight serves. For two years she has watched the Ashmark Pack hunt the very people her code protects, and looked away, knowing she would lose — and it has cost her every time.
```

**J. Anarchist — Nikos Marou**

```
A human man of Greek ancestry — sun-dark olive skin, a mess of black curls, quick dark eyes, 5'9", wiry and dock-hardened, rope-burn scars across both palms.
An Anarchist, and loud about it: "Nobody's born to kneel."
Defiant, irreverent, angry, and fiercely loyal to the downtrodden, he skirts every rank custom he can get away with, eats with the servants, and runs quiet mutual aid for the College's handful of commoners. Given an order, he refuses it. He talks in blunt dockside slang, swears freely, and uses honorifics only to mock them; his humor punctures titles and anyone who takes them seriously. What he keeps from himself is how good it would feel to be obeyed — to sit where the masters sit. A commoner from the docks of Maressa, he took the scholarship because its stipend feeds his family, and stays to learn the power the masters keep for themselves. His loyalty runs to the Empire's common folk, not its Crown; at least they aren't slaves. The Ashmark Pack is nobility at its worst — slavers' children with rank as a license to brutalize — and he has kept clear for two years, knowing commoners who cross them disappear.
```

# Reference

## R1. The World

### Setting
- Victorian-era fantasy. Weapons are blades, bows, polearms, and magic.
- Magic is rare in the general population. Nobles tend to have the strongest magic.
- **Peoples:** Humans, Beastkin (effectively human, with animal ears and a tail; hair color matches fur), Elves, Dwarves, Halflings. Beastkin are named by species: wolfkin, bearkin, tigerkin, foxkin, etc.
- **Calendar:** years are counted from the Empire's founding. The story begins in 782.

### The Aurelian Empire
A union of kingdoms. Each kingdom has its own king and royal house; all are united under the **Emperor**, who is also King of Aurelia. The Eastern March is an Imperial province, held by the Empire itself. Ashmark is the newest member, joined in 748.

- The dominant power.
- Imperial citizens see the people of Ashmark as uncivilized ruffians — but never say it to their faces.
- Beastkin from every other kingdom are treated as normal citizens. The prejudice is against Ashmark, not against beastkin.

### Social Classes (descending)
1. **Royalty** — the Imperial family and the royal houses of the member kingdoms (e.g., House Vargrave)
2. **Nobles** — typically the strongest magic; upper and lesser ranks
3. **Merchants** — wealthy but common-born
4. **Commoners** — the bulk of the population
5. **Slaves** — exist only in Ashmark; mostly prisoners of war, highly mixed — humans, dwarves, elves, other beastkin. The Empire dislikes it but can do nothing about it.

### The Aldwater
The great river. Rises in Ashmark's mountains, spreads into the lakes and wetlands of the Eastern March, then runs west — through Halden, past Aurelis — to the sea at Maressa. It spans many kingdoms and belongs to none.

- The Empire's main artery for barge trade.
- Ashmark's ore and metal travel downriver to Aurelis; the tribute grain travels upriver to Ashmark.
- The river valley is the natural road for an army, in either direction.

### Kingdoms and Province

Culture labels (Irish, French, Persian, etc.) describe each kingdom's culture. Human ancestry is listed separately where it matters.

| Kingdom | Human ancestry |
|---|---|
| Aurelia | every ancestry — the capital draws from everywhere |
| Halden | Irish, and Northwestern European |
| Maressa | darker-skinned Mediterranean |
| Zahrun | Persian |
| Sylvaren | Welsh |
| Durnhald | Scottish, and Norse |
| Eastern March | Eastern European — Polish, Hungarian, and the like |

#### Aurelia (center)
The heartland and founding kingdom; the Emperor rules it as its king. Its capital, **Aurelis**, sits on the Aldwater: the Empire's chief economic hub, fed by barge traffic from east and west. Home of the Royal Magical College.

- **Population:** a mix of every people and beastkin from every kingdom.
- **Aesthetic:** Victorian — stone and wrought iron, gaslit streets, grand halls and spires.
- **Fashion:** Victorian clothing — tailored coats, waistcoats, high collars, corsetry, and full skirts.

#### Halden (east-central)
The Breadbasket: an Irish shire-country kingdom of open plains and rolling hills behind the Eastern March, home to humans, halfling shires, and beastkin — mousekin, sheepkin, rabbitkin, dogkin, catkin, squirrelkin, foxkin, and bearkin. The Aldwater runs through it, and irrigation channels spread from the river. The Empire's grain.

- Raided by Ashmark for generations before 748.
- The Empire buys its grain to pay Ashmark's tribute — the people who suffered the raids now feed the raiders.
- Its old fear is the deep freeze: raids came in winter.

#### The Eastern March (east)
An Imperial province, owned by the Empire rather than any kingdom — the Empire's side of the borderlands. A country of wetlands and lakes where the Aldwater comes down from the mountains.

- **Population:** a mix from every kingdom.

- The fortified border facing Ashmark: keeps on the causeways and crossings. The garrisons failed to hold the line — Ashmark broke through, and that is how it won the treaty.
- The wetlands are a natural wall — an army can only cross on the causeways, so whoever holds the keeps holds the border.
- Raiding season was winter: when the lakes froze, the wall disappeared.

#### Ashmark (far east)
The Beast Kingdom, east of the Empire — the far side of the borderlands. Its western half, facing the Eastern March, is cold mountains, where the Aldwater rises and the mines are. Its eastern half is badlands, running out toward the hordes.

- **Ruler:** King Fenrick Vargrave, "the Inferno" (wolfkin), of House Vargrave — a royal house in which every member is wolfkin.
  - A warlord king who conquered the borderland tribes and united them as the Ashborn. The Empire decided it was better to bring him in than fight him, and Ashmark joined as an allied / confederate state in 748.
  - Feared and respected across the Empire. A powerful fire mage who runs a brutal hold and burns his enemies alive.
  - His wife was killed ten years ago (772) in a border clash with the barbarian horde tribes of the far east. He earned the title "the Inferno" in his retaliation: he burned the tribes responsible.
- **Militaristic.** Takes slaves and burns its enemies alive.
- **Population:** predatory beastkin — wolf, big cats, dog, bear, badger, fox, and other canid species — all of Mongolian ancestry in appearance. The rest are slaves: mostly prisoners of war, highly mixed — humans, dwarves, elves, other beastkin.
- **Economy:** mines metals in the mountains, and harvests silk from giant spiders. Can't feed itself.
- **Fashion:** Chinese-inspired finery — flowing, beautiful and elegant, yet combat-ready.
- **The Ashborn:** Ashmark's free citizens — everyone who is not a slave. Proud, and almost always display their silks.
- **Food:** Ashmark's military once raided the Empire for food. The Empire now supplies it as tribute — the price of bringing Ashmark in and preventing further conflict.

#### Beyond Ashmark (farthest east)
The barbarian hordes.

#### Maressa (west)
The coastal kingdom at the Aldwater's mouth. Port, navy, and sea trade.

- **Population:** mostly human, with elves and a mix of every kind of beastkin.
- **Culture:** French-inspired and artistic — the Empire's home of painters, sculptors, salons, and fashion houses.

#### Zahrun (southwest)
The desert kingdom. Persian in culture.

- **Population:** mostly human, with some desert beastkin — fennec foxkin, jackalkin, and desert cats.
- **Economy:** precious gems and coveted spices.

#### Sylvaren (south)
The elven kingdom of the southern forests. Celtic and druidic in culture.

- **Population:** mostly elves, with some foxkin and rabbitkin.
- **Economy:** exotic pets, enchanted gear, and moon flowers — ground to make magic essence.

#### Durnhald (north)
The dwarven kingdom of the northern holds. Scottish highland in culture. Temperate and rainy; timber and mines.

- **Population:** mostly dwarves, with some beastkin — wolfkin, bearkin, and boarkin.
- **Economy:** raw timber, ores, ironworks, weapons and armor, and siege weapons and contraptions.

### Monsters and Ruins
- Monster populations live throughout the kingdoms — standard fantasy fare: kobolds, goblins, dire animals, magical beasts, and the like.
- Ancient ruins — dungeons — left by long-forgotten races lie scattered across the land.
- The College's knight path trains on both: monster hunts and dungeon delves.

### After the College
Where graduates go:
1. **Imperial Knight Orders** — the Emperor's own knights.
2. **The Eastern March** — the Imperial border garrison.
3. **Kingdom service** — knights in their own king's service.
4. **House retainers** — leading or serving a noble House's forces.
5. **Delvers and adventurers** — free companies that hunt monsters and delve ruins.
### Ashmark and the Empire
- The Empire's food keeps Ashmark at peace; Ashmark's peace keeps the Empire's border safe.
- At the College, Cindra outranks nearly every student as First Princess of Ashmark, and rank custom says students obey her.
- Punishing Ashmark's heir risks the peace, so the faculty and the Imperial nobility look away from what the Ashmark Pack does.

## R2. Magic

- Magic runs on mana, through mana channels. Mana is the only limit: casting costs nothing physical, and fatigue sets in only below about 10% of capacity.
- Magic rewards freedom and creativity — clever, unconventional uses are encouraged.
- **Common arts** every student learns: Warding (shields, barriers), Mana Reinforcement (empowering body, weapon, armor), Mana Sense (feeling and reading magic), Mana Projection (raw force), Dispelling (unraveling spells), Imbuing (channeling one's domain into a weapon), among others.

### Magic Domains
Each character has one primary domain.

- **Storm** — Lightning, Wind, Water
- **Tide** — Water, Ice, Shadow
- **Mountain** — Stone, Metal, Ice
- **Ash** — Fire, Wind, Chemical
- **Infernal** — Unholy, Fire, Shadow
- **Radiant** — Holy, Light, Healing
- **Verdant** — Plants, Healing, Poison
- **Void** — Spatial, Shadow, Gravity
- **Lunar** — Illusion, Shadow, Light
- **Tundra** — Ice, Wind, Stone
- **Dusk** — Shadow, Sound, Poison

## R6. Infinite Worlds Setup (for the game author)

### Infinite Worlds tracked items

Each character carries one `initialTrackedItemValues` entry per character-based tracked item. A plain string is a fixed value; a list is a dropdown whose first item is the default.

- **Sex** — male characters: fixed `"Male"`. Female characters: dropdown `["Female", "Female Futa"]`.
- **Primary Martial** — dropdown, the character's weapon first, then the rest.
- **Primary Magic Domain** — dropdown, the character's domain first, then the rest.
- **Preferred Fashion** — fixed string, the character's own style (auto-updates in play).
- **Cindra's Sex** — global, set once by the player (`Female` / `Female Futa`); no per-character entry.
